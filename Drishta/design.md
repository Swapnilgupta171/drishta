# DRISHTA — Design Direction

> **Seeing through the eyes of artists.**  
> An immersive exploration of human creativity in an age of artificial intelligence.

## 1. Design intent

Drishta should feel like entering an artist's imagination—not visiting a conventional SaaS landing page. The experience is surreal, poetic, sophisticated and unmistakably visual. Its design should communicate that art carries memory, emotion, intention, experience and individual perspective.

The website is a conceptual product showcase. It explains Drishta's philosophy; it must not pretend to detect AI art or make unsupported claims.

## 2. Style hierarchy

- **Surrealism — imagination (dominant):** dreamlike environments, impossible landscapes, floating forms, symbolic imagery and unexpected but purposeful compositions.
- **Art Nouveau — identity (supporting):** organic curves, botanical ornament, flowing lines, decorative frames, elegant typography and natural motifs.
- **Paper Cutout — human touch (accent):** layered forms, tactile paper grain, imperfect edges and handcrafted depth. Use selectively; do not turn the site into a scrapbook.

These styles have different jobs. Do not mix them randomly or give them equal visual weight.

## 3. Atmosphere and palette

Aim for mysterious, warm, contemplative and richly illustrated—not childish, cluttered or overly vintage.

Suggested palette:
- Deep forest green and dark teal for depth.
- Muted sage and stone for quieter surfaces.
- Parchment and warm natural neutrals for contrast.
- Antique gold for fine ornament and emphasis.
- Restrained burgundy and midnight blue for dramatic accents.

Do not default to a cream-and-orange AI-template palette. Avoid decorative gradients.

## 4. Typography

Use an expressive display serif for major headings and a highly readable complementary face for body copy. Typography should feel editorial and integrated with the artwork. Avoid Inter, gimmicky painted headline highlights and excessive decorative type. Preserve readability and sensible line lengths.

## 5. Composition and interface

- Prefer asymmetric, editorial compositions over centring every element.
- Use deliberate whitespace and strong visual hierarchy.
- Establish one consistent corner-radius system.
- Avoid repetitive three-card feature sections and unnecessary pill badges.
- Do not use emoji icons; use considered SVG artwork or a restrained icon set.
- Keep navigation clear and unobtrusive.
- Let illustrations, typography and composition do the expressive work; do not decorate every empty space.

The result should have artistic complexity with compositional discipline.

## 6. Visitor journey — six connected chapters

The site is a single-page illustrated journey. Chapters should feel connected, not like six unrelated template sections.

1. **The World of Drishta** — An arresting entrance, distinctive wordmark, concise brand statement and a surreal hero composition.
2. **The Human Perspective** — Explore memory, emotion, imagination, experience and intention through visual metaphors.
3. **Two Ways of Creating** — Thoughtfully explore human-created and AI-generated imagery, focusing on authorship and process rather than declaring one inherently superior.
4. **What Makes Art Human?** — Give imperfection, experimentation, the artist's hand and individual expression a visually rich treatment.
5. **The Drishta Philosophy** — Explain the conceptual product: recognising artistic origins and valuing human authorship. This is explanatory, not a functioning detector.
6. **Preserve the Human Essence** — Resolve the visual journey with a memorable brand statement and a calm, strong ending.

## 7. Content and integrity

Use concise, evocative copy. Avoid generic marketing language, fabricated statistics, fake testimonials, invented customer logos or claims of verified detection accuracy. Do not imply the conceptual product has capabilities that have not been built.

## 8. Responsive and accessible design

The visual identity must survive on mobile and tablet, not merely shrink from desktop. Recompose illustrations and typography intentionally for small screens. Keep navigation usable, maintain contrast, provide meaningful alt text and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 9. Quality test

Before calling the design complete, ask:
- Does it feel like one coherent imagined world?
- Is surrealism clearly dominant, with Art Nouveau supporting it?
- Is the paper-cut influence subtle and tactile?
- Are layouts varied but still coherent?
- Is every decorative element earning its place?
- Does the story remain understandable without animation?
- Does the product copy remain honest about its conceptual status?
